	3.1 Honest-advice box (styled as a highlighted callout)
H3: When you don't need electropolished tube
Many dairy, water and food lines don't. 3-A and EHEDG hygienic-design guidance sets product-contact surfaces at Ra 0.8 µm (32 µin) or smoother, and research on food-plant cleaning finds little benefit from going much smoother. A good mechanically polished sanitary tube meets that at lower cost. Electropolishing earns its price in pharmaceutical, hospital and laboratory water systems, where corrosion resistance and a passive, low-particle surface matter. Send us your specification and we'll tell you honestly which one it calls for — even when that means the cheaper tube.

4.1 How we make electropolished tube (anchor #process)
Eyebrow: OUR PROCESS
H2: How We Make Electropolished Tube
Intro: Electropolishing is the last finishing step, not a repair. It improves a good tube; it cannot rescue a poor one. That is why each step before it matters.
Process steps (7) — step title and explanation
1. Start from sound base tube. We start from [welded ASTM A270 tube / seamless tube — client to confirm]. A deep scratch, lap or weld pit that exists before electropolishing is still there after it.
2. Anneal. [Bright / solution annealing — client to confirm] restores corrosion resistance after forming and welding.
3. Mechanically pre-polish the bore. For SF4, ASME BPE calls for a mechanical polish followed by electropolishing. The pre-polish sets the base profile; electropolishing then levels it.
4. Electropolish the bore. The tube becomes the anode, a cathode runs through the bore, and an acid electrolyte [phosphoric/sulfuric-acid based — client to confirm] dissolves the surface. Peaks dissolve faster than valleys, so the profile levels. Free iron and embedded particles go with it, leaving a chromium-enriched passive layer.
5. Rinse and dry. DI-water rinse and [filtered nitrogen drying — client to confirm], so no electrolyte or water spots remain inside.
6. Inspect. Internal Ra on a profilometer and a visual check of the bore against the ordered finish (full plan in section 5.4).
7. Protect. End caps, poly sleeve and ISPM-15 crate, so the finish arrives the way it left.
The YouTube video goes directly under these steps — see section 8.
Callout box — H3: What electropolishing cannot do
* It won't remove deep defects. A pit or lap in the base tube shows through the finish.
* It doesn't change the grade. 304L electropolished is still 304L; pick the grade for the chemistry first.
* It doesn't make a system rouge-proof. Hot WFI and clean-steam systems can still form rouge over time, so plan derouging and repassivation into maintenance.

5.2 Mistakes we see in RFQs (Experience)
[Client's sales desk to confirm each point from their own RFQs; delete any they haven't actually seen.]
H3: Five Specification Mistakes We See Most Often
* "Electropolished" with no SF designation. Two suppliers can quote very different finishes against the same word.
* Pipe schedules for a sanitary line. Sanitary clamps and fittings are made for OD tube, not NPS Sch 10S pipe.
* 316 instead of 316L on welded systems. The low-carbon L grade protects weld zones from sensitisation.
* No sulfur range on tube that meets another supplier's fittings. A sulfur mismatch between the two sides can pull the orbital weld pool off-centre.
* OD polish on tube where only the bore touches product. It adds cost with no process benefit.

5.6 Storage on an Al Ain site
H3: Storing Electropolished Tube in Al Ain's Heat and Dust
Intro: Heat and wind-blown dust do the most damage between delivery and installation. [Client to confirm these match the handling advice they give.]
* Keep end caps and poly sleeves on until the tube is cut for installation; they keep dust and sand out of the bore.
* Store under cover, out of direct sun. Heat and UV weaken plastic caps and sleeves.
* Rack tubes off the ground on timber or plastic supports, never directly on carbon steel. Iron pickup from carbon steel shows up later as rust spots.
* Handle with clean gloves, and use only stainless-steel tools on the bore.
* Store 316L and 304L apart, with heat-number markings visible for traceability.
